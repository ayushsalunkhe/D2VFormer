SECTION 0 — Computer & Data Basics

Chalo shuru karte hain bilkul ground zero se! Agar tumne zindagi mein kabhi Machine Learning ya Data Science ka ek lafz bhi na padha ho, tab bhi yeh book tumhe sab kuch step-by-step sikha degi. Sabse pehle samajhte hain ki "Data" hota kya hai.

0.1 Data, Table, Rows aur Columns kya hote hain?

Simple words mein, Data ka matlab hota hai koi bhi information (jaankari) jo hum collect karke record karte hain. Jab hum is information ko computer mein store karte hain, toh hum use aksar ek Table (Excel sheet jaisi) mein organize karte hain.

Ek Aam Table Ka Example: Student Data

Roll NoStudent NameMarksCity

103Amit78Pune

Is table mein dhyan se dekho:

  • Row (Line / Record): Ek horizontal line (jaise: 101 | Rahul | 85 | Delhi). Yeh ek single entity yaani ek student ki poori jaankari hai.
  • Column (Attribute / Property): Ek vertical line (jaise: Marks). Yeh har student ki ek specific quality ya property batata hai.
  • Feature (Input Variable): Machine Learning mein hum Column ko aksar Feature kehte hain. Yeh woh information hoti hai jise dekh kar computer kuch samajhta hai ya predict karta hai.
  • Target (Output Variable): Jis cheez ko hum predict karna chahte hain, use hum Target ya Label bolte hain. Example ke liye, agar hum student ke study hours dekh kar uske Marks predict karna chahte hain, toh study hours Feature hai aur Marks Target hai.

0.2 Time Series Data aam Data se alag kyun hota hai?

Ab sabse bada sawaal: Kya time series data bhi aisi hi ordinary table hoti hai? Nahi! Yahan ek bohot bada farq hota hai.

Upar di gayi Student table mein agar tum Rahul aur Priya ki row ko aapas mein swap (ulta-pulta) kar do, toh kya table ka matlab badal jayega? Bilkul nahi! Rahul ke marks ab bhi 85 rahenge aur Priya ke 92. Yaani normal tabular data mein order (kram) matter nahi karta.

Lekin ab is table ko dekho:

Technical Definition: Timestamp & Sequence

Timestamp: Ek specific date aur time jab koi reading measure ki gayi (e.g., 2022-04-06 09:00:00).

Sequence: Data points ki ek aisi ordered chain jisme har agla point apne pichle point ke waqt par depend karta hai. Isme sequence ka order todne se poora meaning destroy ho jata hai.

SECTION 1 — What is a Time Series?

1.1 Time Series ka Aasaan Matlab

Time Series Data ka seedha matlab hai: "Aise measurements jo waqt ke sath barabar intervals (regular intervals) par liye gaye hon."

Real-life mein tum time series data har jagah dekhte ho:

  • Weather (Mausam): Har ghante ka taapmaan (Temperature every hour).
  • Electricity (Bijli): Har 15 minute mein grid se kitni bijli consume hui.

  • Finance: Stock market mein TCS ya Reliance ke share ki har minute ki keemat.
  • Health (ECG): Heartbeat ka graph jo har millisecond record hota hai.

1.2 Forecasting kya hoti hai?

English mein Forecast ka matlab hota hai Bhavishyavani (Prediction of the future). Jab hum pichle (historical) observations ko dekh kar aane wale waqt (future observations) ka andaza lagate hain, use Time-Series Forecasting kehte hain.

Ek Simple Story: Dhoop aur Taapmaan

Maan lo tum pichle 4 ghante se temperature observe kar rahe ho:

  • Subah 6 baje: 18°C
  • Subah 7 baje: 20°C
  • Subah 8 baje: 23°C
  • Subah 9 baje: 26°C

Agar main tumse poochun: "Subah 10 baje kitna temperature hoga?" Toh tumhara dimaag kahega: "Suraj nikal raha hai, taapmaan lagataar badh raha hai, toh 10 baje shayad 28°C ya 29°C hoga."

Tumne yahan do cheezein use keen:

  1. Historical Trend: Pichle data ka badhta hua trend.
  2. Domain Knowledge / Time Context: Tumhe pata hai ki subah dhoop aane par garmi badhti hai.

Hamara machine learning model (D2Vformer) bhi computer ke andar exactly yahi karne ki koshish karta hai!

3.1 Dataset 1: ETTh1 (Electricity Transformer Temperature)

ETTh1 ka full form hai Electricity Transformer Temperature (Hourly 1). Yeh time-series machine learning research ka sabse famous "Gold Standard" benchmark dataset hai. Jaise computer vision mein MNIST ya CIFAR-10 hota hai, waise hi time series forecasting mein ETT benchmark hota hai.

ETTh1 Specifications

  • Origin: China ke do alag-alag counties mein electricity transformers se collect kiya gaya tha.
  • Duration: 2 saal ka continuous data (July 2016 se July 2018).
  • Frequency: Har 1 ghante mein ek reading (Hourly frequency). Total 17,420 rows.
  • Features (Total 7 channels):
    1. HUFL — High UseFull Load (Transformer ka high-voltage side ka useful electrical load)
    2. HULL — High UseLess Load (High-voltage side ka reactive / useless load)
    3. MUFL — Middle UseFull Load (Middle-voltage side useful load)
    4. MULL — Middle UseLess Load (Middle-voltage side useless load)
    5. LUFL — Low UseFull Load (Low-voltage side useful load)
    6. LULL — Low UseLess Load (Low-voltage side useless load)
    7. OT — Oil Temperature (TARGET): Transformer cooling oil ka temperature. Yeh sabse critical feature hai!

Viva Tip: Training vs Inference

Examiner: "What is the difference between training and inference?"

Answer: "Training is the heavy, slow process where model weights are updated using gradient descent and backpropagation over multiple epochs on GPU. Inference is the fast, read-only phase where a frozen trained checkpoint takes an input and outputs a prediction in a few milliseconds without updating any weights."

5.2 StandardScaler (Z-Score Normalization)

Is problem ko solve karne ke liye hum StandardScaler use karte hain. Yeh har feature ko transform karta hai taaki:

  • Har feature ka Mean (Average) = 0 ho jaye.
  • Har feature ka Standard Deviation (Spread) = 1 ho jaye.

Mathematical Formula for Z-Score:

           x - μ
     z = ---------
             σ

Jahan:
x = Original raw value (e.g., PM2.5 = 200 µg/m³)
μ (Mu) = Training set ka Mean (Average value)
σ (Sigma) = Training set ka Standard Deviation (Variance ka square root)
z = Normalized value (jo aksar -2.0 se +2.0 ke beech hoti hai)

SECTION 7 — Evaluation Metrics

Examiner poochega: "How did you know your model was doing well? What metric did you measure?"

7.1 The Big Three: MSE, MAE, RMSE

1. Mean Squared Error (MSE)

Concept: Prediction aur Actual ke beech ke difference (error) ka square nikalo, aur sabka average le lo.

         1    N
  MSE = ---  ∑  (Actual_i - Predicted_i)²
         N   i=1
  • Square kyun karte hain? Do reasons: (1) Negative errors positive ban jayein (-5 aur +5 cancel na hon), aur (2) Badi galtiyon par bohot bhaari penalty lage (agar 10 unit ka error hai toh square hoke 100 ban jayega).

2. Mean Absolute Error (MAE)

Concept: Prediction aur Actual ke beech ke absolute (positive) difference ka direct average.

         1    N
  MAE = ---  ∑  |Actual_i - Predicted_i|
         N   i=1
  • Kyun zaroori hai? Yeh hume aasan bhasha mein batata hai: "On an average, hamara model kitne units door hai." Agar MAE = 14.2 µg/m³ hai, iska matlab average galti 14.2 unit ki hai.

3. Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE)

Concept: MSE ka Square Root le lo: RMSE = √(MSE).

Iska fayda yeh hai ki yeh wapas usi original unit mein aa jata hai jisme data tha (e.g., °C ya µg/m³).

Rule of Thumb

LOWER IS BETTER! Chahe MSE ho, MAE ho, ya RMSE ho — yeh sab "Galtiyan" (Errors) hain. Jitna chota number hoga, model utna hi zyada accurate hoga!

7.2 Crucial Concept: Normalized vs Denormalized Metrics

Yeh point bohot dhyan se padho, kyunki tumhare Streamlit screenshots mein dono numbers aate hain aur agar examiner ne pooch liya toh bacche confuse ho jaate hain:

FeatureNormalized Test Metrics (Paper Style)Denormalized Window Metrics (Streamlit Live)
Scale Z-score normalized space (-2.0 se +2.0 range) Original physical units (e.g., PM2.5 in µg/m³, Oil Temp in °C)
Scope Poore test set ke hazaaron windows ka average Screen par dikh rahe ek single active window (4 din) ka error
Typical Value (Delhi AQI) 0.1179 (Normalized MSE) 332.25 (Denormalized MSE in µg²/m⁶)
Kyun alag hain? Kyunki raw value me (210 - 190)² = 400 hota hai, jabki normalized z-score me (0.5 - 0.3)² = 0.04 hota hai! Dono bilkul sahi hain, sirf scale ka farq hai.

SECTION 8 — Why Normal Models Have a Problem

Ab hum D2Vformer ke sabse core motivation par aate hain: "Aakhir humne D2Vformer ko chuna kyun? Existing models mein aisi kya kami thi jo D2Vformer ko banana pada?"

8.1 Problem 1: Fixed Prediction Horizon Rigidness

Pehle ke jitne bhi standard time-series deep learning models the (jaise standard Informer, Autoformer, PatchTST, Fedformer, ya LSTM) — unka architecture rigid (sakht) hota hai.

The Fixed Projection Matrix Problem

Traditional models mein ek final layer hoti hai jo prediction generate karti hai: Linear(seq_len, pred_len).

Agar tumne model ko 96 hours ke liye train kiya hai, toh uski aakhri layer ka matrix dimension fixed ho gaya: (96, 96).

Ab kal ko tumhara user ya manager aake bole:

"Sun bhai, mujhe 96 ghante nahi, sirf aage ke 48 ghante ka forecast chahiye!"

Toh traditional model bolta hai:

"Mera matrix 96 hours ke liye fixed hai. Main 48 hours directly nahi de sakta. Tum mujhe 48 hours ke naye dataset par scratch se dobara train karo!"

Aur agar user bole: "Mujhe 336 hours (2 hafte) ka chahiye?" Toh phir se naya model banao aur phir se ghanton train karo!

8.2 Problem 2: Relative vs Absolute Time (Calendar Blindness)

Purane models sequence ko aise dekhte hain:

Index 0 -> Index 1 -> Index 2 -> ... -> Index 95

Unhe yeh pata hota hai ki Step 2, Step 1 ke baad aata hai. Lekin unhe yeh BILKUL NAHI PATA HOTA ki:

  • Step 1 asal mein Monday Subah 9:00 AM tha!
  • Step 25 asal mein Tuesday Subah 9:00 AM tha!
  • Yeh dono steps alag-alag din ke same rush-hour traffic ko represent karte hain!

SECTION 9 — What is a Transformer?

Agar tumne Transformer ka naam sirf ChatGPT ke context mein suna hai, toh chalo isko zero se samajhte hain.

9.1 The Classroom Story: Query, Key, Value

Transformer ka sabse powerful engine hota hai Attention Mechanism. Isko ek aasan story se samjho:

Library / Classroom Analogy

Socho tum ek library mein gaye ho:

  • Query (Q): Tumhara sawaal! Tum library ke librarian se kehte ho: "Mujhe Indian History par kitaab chahiye." (Yeh tumhari Query hai).
  • Key (K): Library ki almariyon par lage labels! Ek shelf par likha hai: "Science", doosre par "Indian History", teesre par "Cooking". Librarian tumhari Query ko har shelf ki Key ke sath match karta hai.
  • Attention Score: Similarity match! "Indian History" shelf ka score 99% aayega, jabki "Cooking" ka score 1% aayega.
  • Value (V): Us shelf ke andar rakhi hui actual kitaabein! Librarian us shelf se kitaab nikal kar tumhe de deta hai.

Mathematical Attention Formula

                                     ( Q · Kᵀ )
      Attention(Q, K, V) = Softmax  ( -------- ) · V
                                     (   √d_k  )

Matlab:
1. Q aur K ka dot product nikalo (similarity check karo).
2. √d_k se divide karo taaki numbers bohot bade na hon (stability).
3. Softmax lagao taaki probabilities 0 se 1 ke beech aa jayein (Attention Weights).
4. Un weights se Value (V) ko multiply karke weighted sum le lo!

SECTION 10 — What is Positional Information?

10.1 "Step 1, Step 2" vs "Monday 9 AM"

Transformer architecture basically parallel processing karta hai. Lekin time-series mein agar position na batayi jaye, toh model ko pata hi nahi chalega ki kaunsa data pehle aaya aur kaunsa baad mein.

Traditional NLP (Natural Language Processing) mein hum Positional Encoding (PE) use karte the: [Pos 0, Pos 1, Pos 2...]. Lekin time series mein sirf index kaafi nahi hota. Calendar ka ek cyclic nature hota hai:

  • Har 24 ghante baad raat ke 12 baje cycle repeat hoti hai.
  • Har 7 din baad Sunday wapas aata hai.
  • Har 12 mahine baad January wapas aati hai.

D2Vformer isi calendar periodicity ko use karta hai through Date2Vec!

SECTION 11 — Date2Vec (Deep Dive)

Yeh section tumhare project ka Heart (Dil) hai! Agar examiner ne Date2Vec par sawaal poocha aur tumne acche se explain kar diya, toh full marks pakke hain.

11.1 Date2Vec Ka Core Idea Kya Hai?

Date2Vec ka kaam hai kisi bhi calendar timestamp (e.g., 2022-04-06 09:00:00) ko ek mathematical dense vector (numbers ki ek chain) mein convert karna.

Yeh vector char main calendar features se milkar banta hai:

  1. Hour: Din ka kaunsa ghanta hai (0 se 23). Normalized to [-0.5, 0.5].
  2. Weekday: Hafte ka kaunsa din hai (Monday=0 se Sunday=6). Normalized.
  3. Day: Mahine ka kaunsa din hai (1 se 31). Normalized.
  4. Month: Saal ka kaunsa mahina hai (1 se 12). Normalized.

11.2 Sine aur Cosine / Fourier Analysis Intuitively

Computer ke liye 23:00 (raat ke 11 baje) aur 00:00 (raat ke 12 baje) normal numbers mein bohot door hain (23 vs 0). Lekin ghadi mein dekho toh dono bagal mein hain (sirf 1 ghante ka farq)!

Clock / Circle Analogy: Sine aur Cosine

Imagine karo ek gol ghadi (Circle). Circle mein ghoomte hue jab tum 360 degree complete karte ho, toh wapas zero par aa jaate ho. Trigonometric functions (Sine aur Cosine) ki yahi khoobi hoti hai ki yeh smoothly repeat karte hain:

         sin(0) = 0,   sin(π/2) = 1,   sin(π) = 0,   sin(3π/2) = -1,   sin(2π) = 0 (Full Circle)

Date2Vec Fourier decomposition use karta hai: Yeh raw time signals ko alag-alag frequencies ke sine/cosine waves ke linear combination mein break karta hai. Isse model ko din, hafte, aur saal ki cyclic waves bilkul crystal-clear samajh aati hain!

SECTION 13 — Patching Mechanism

13.1 Patching Kya Hoti Hai?

Computer Vision mein Vision Transformer (ViT) images ko 16x16 ke chhote tukdon (patches) mein kaatta hai. Time series mein bhi hum continuous data points ko single-single dekhne ke bajaye chhote-chhote patches (windows) mein dekhte hain.

Hamare Project Ke Parameters:

  • seq_len = 96 (Total 96 ghante ka data)
  • patch_len = 16 (Ek patch mein 16 ghante ka data hota hai)
  • stride = 8 (Agla patch pichle patch se 8 ghante aage shift hota hai)

Overlapping Patches: Kyunki stride=8 patch_len=16 se chota hai, har agla patch pichle patch ke 8 ghanton ke sath 50% overlap karta hai! Isse information boundary par cut nahi hoti aur temporal continuity bani rehti hai.

SECTION 14 — The Fusion Block

Yeh D2Vformer ka sabse smart component hai. Yahi flexible prediction ko possible banata hai!

14.1 Intuition: Future Timestamp as a Query

Socho hume Next Monday Subah 9:00 AM ka PM2.5 predict karna hai:

  1. Model sabse pehle agale Monday 9 AM ka timestamp Date2Vec mein daalta hai.
  2. Date2Vec us timestamp ka ek feature vector banata hai. Yeh banta hai hamara Query (Q)!
  3. Model pichle data ke paas jaata hai: "Mere paas past 96 hours ke jo Date2Vec vectors hain (Keys - K), unme se kaunsa waqt agale Monday 9 AM se match karta hai?"
  4. Model ko dikhta hai ki pichle hafte ka Monday morning aur daily morning hours bohot high attention weight score receive karte hain!
  5. Model un past patterns ka weighted sum (Value - V) nikal kar future prediction generate kar deta hai!

Viva Gold Answer: How is D2Vformer flexible?

"In traditional models, the output sequence length is hardcoded into a linear projection matrix. In D2Vformer, future predictions are generated by cross-attending future timestamp embeddings (Date2Vec queries) against historical data in the Fusion Block. Therefore, to change the forecast horizon from 48h to 336h, we simply supply the required number of future timestamp queries. The trained model weights remain completely unchanged."

21.2 Yeh Asal Mein Kaam Kaise Karta Hai?

Iska secret D2Vformer ke architectural decouple design mein hai:

  1. Learned Weights Are Frozen: Date2Vec ke weights aur Attention projection matrices frozen rehte hain (checkpoint load hone ke baad koi backprop nahi hota).
  2. Dynamic Future Querying: Jab user slider se horizon select karta hai (jaise 192h), toh model decoder ko 192 future timestamps deta hai: x_mark_dec[:, -192:, :].
  3. Fusion Cross-Attention: Fusion Block in 192 future timestamp embeddings ko query bana kar historical data par attention calculate karta hai aur 192 time steps ka forecast de deta hai!

SECTION 22 — DLinear Multi-Horizon Comparison

22.1 Why Did DLinear Need 10 Checkpoints?

DLinear mein projection layer direct linear mapping karti hai: nn.Linear(seq_len=96, pred_len=H).

Iska matlab yeh hai ki output dimension H ke sath weight tensor ka shape change ho jata hai:

  • Horizon 48h ke liye linear matrix ka size: (48, 96)
  • Horizon 72h ke liye linear matrix ka size: (72, 96)
  • Horizon 96h ke liye linear matrix ka size: (96, 96)
  • Horizon 192h ke liye linear matrix ka size: (192, 96)
  • Horizon 336h ke liye linear matrix ka size: (336, 96)

Ek 48h ke trained DLinear checkpoint mein 192h ya 336h ka data flow ho hi nahi sakta kyunki tensor dimension crash ho jayega! Isliye DLinear ke liye hume 10 alag-alag model train karne pade (5 for ETTh1 + 5 for Delhi AQI).
